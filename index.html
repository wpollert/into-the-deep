Into the Deep

The ocean covers 71% of our planet, and we've explored only a small part of it. Scroll down to dive from the waves to the deepest point on Earth.

0 – 200 m · Epipelagic

The Sunlight Zone

Sunlight reaches this layer, so most of the ocean's life lives here. Tiny phytoplankton drift near the surface and make much of the oxygen in the air you breathe.

🌿

Ocean-powered breathing

Marine plants and plankton produce roughly half of Earth's oxygen.

🐢

Ancient navigators

Sea turtles migrate thousands of kilometres and return to the beach where they hatched.

🪸

Rainforests of the sea

Coral reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor but support about a quarter of all marine species.

🐋
🦈
🪼
200 – 1,000 m · Mesopelagic

The Twilight Zone

Down here the light fades to a dim blue glow, and nothing can photosynthesise. Every night, many of the animals here swim up to the surface to feed.

🌙

The biggest migration on Earth

Billions of animals rise toward the surface every night and sink back down at dawn.

🐋

Deep-diving giants

Sperm whales dive past 1,000 m hunting squid and can hold their breath for over an hour.

🪼

Glass bodies

Many creatures here are see-through, which makes them nearly invisible in the dim light.

1,000 – 4,000 m · Bathypelagic

The Midnight Zone

No sunlight reaches this depth. The only light comes from the animals themselves. Move your cursor (or tap) to shine your torch into the dark.

🔦 Your flashlight is on
✨

Living light

About three-quarters of deep-sea animals make their own light through bioluminescence.

🎣

Fishing with a lantern

Anglerfish dangle a glowing lure, full of light-making bacteria, to draw prey toward their jaws.

🦑

Eyes the size of plates

Giant squid have some of the largest eyes in the animal kingdom, up to about 27 cm across.

🐙
🪱
4,000 – 6,000 m · Abyssopelagic

The Abyss

Near-freezing water, total darkness and crushing pressure. Plenty of animals still live here, surviving on "marine snow", the bits of food that drift down from above.

🐙

Dumbo octopus

Named after the flying elephant, it flaps its ear-like fins to glide and lives deeper than any other octopus.

❄️

Always cold

The water here stays around 1–4 °C all year round.

🌋

Life without sunlight

Around hydrothermal vents, whole ecosystems run on chemical energy instead of sunlight.

6,000 – 10,935 m · Hadal

Challenger Deep

You've reached the bottom of the Mariana Trench, the deepest known point in the ocean. If you dropped Mount Everest here, its peak would still be more than 2 km underwater.
